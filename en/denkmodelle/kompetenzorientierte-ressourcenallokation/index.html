Thinking models

Competency-based resource allocation

Looking at resources not only in terms of existing positions, but in terms of competencies, tasks and actual needs.

01Initial problem

The model addresses the question of how existing staff resources can be deployed more sensibly and existing competencies used better.

02Guiding question

Which competencies are available – which competencies are needed – and how can the two be brought together more effectively?

03Model

  1. Existing resources→
  2. Competency profile→
  3. Organisational needs→
  4. Matching→
  5. Gap→
  6. Options

Possible options: transfer, further development, change of tasks, new forms of cooperation, new hiring, another organisational solution.

04Elements

  • existing employees
  • qualifications
  • experience
  • skills
  • tasks
  • capacities
  • organisational needs
  • development potential
  • costs
  • time requirements

05Example

An organisation needs additional capacity in a particular area of work. At the same time, there are employees internally with suitable core competencies who currently mainly carry out other tasks.

It can then first be examined whether an internal change would make economic and organisational sense.

06Possible impact

Decisions about resources are considered not only in terms of headcount and costs, but also in terms of competencies, needs and impact.

07Limitations

The model is a business and organisational review and not employment law advice.
